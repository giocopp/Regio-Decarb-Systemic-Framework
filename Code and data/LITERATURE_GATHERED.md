## A. Composite-indicator aggregation (the + vs × / non-compensatory choice)

- **Nardo, Saisana, Saltelli & Tarantola (2008).** *Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators — Methodology and User Guide.* OECD/JRC. [PDF](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/jrc47008_handbook_final.pdf) `[WEB]` — canonical reference for compensatory (additive) vs non-compensatory (geometric/multiplicative) aggregation; also data transformation/normalisation of skewed indicators (supports the log step).
- **Greco, Ishizaka, Tasiou & Torrisi (2018).** *On the methodological framework of composite indices: weighting, aggregation, robustness.* Social Indicators Research. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/1446d7b52cf9532193235a86005aa799/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — review anchor for aggregation choices.
- **Munda & Nardo (2009).** *Noncompensatory/nonlinear composite indicators for ranking countries.* Applied Economics. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/1b0a312436e75af99abe772c8d3dadbc/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — foundations of non-compensatory aggregation.
- **Munda & Saisana (2011).** *Methodological considerations on regional sustainability assessment.* Regional Studies. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/08982a1b36625d64871323757ee867ab/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — non-compensatory aggregation on **EU regional** composites.
- **Rogge (2017).** *Composite indicators as generalized benefit-of-the-doubt weighted averages.* Eur. J. Operational Research. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4d4de7dfc4915bf78656c3cde5e7b5cf/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — multiplicative/generalized-mean aggregation.
- **Dialga & Giang (2017).** *Highlighting methodological limitations in composite-indicator construction.* Social Indicators Research. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/65eac637bf0759ad9b9495953ef372dc/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — geometric vs linear changes rankings (motivates sensitivity).
- **Mariani et al. (2022).** *Aggregating composite indicators through the geometric mean: a penalization approach.* Computation. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/e914f2e747a859ba87705d782f6a9eee/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — remedy if geometric mean over-penalises low components.

## B. IPCC AR6 risk framework (conjunctive / multiplicative risk)

- **Simpson et al. (2021).** *A framework for complex climate change risk assessment.* One Earth. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7870bcc2bfe75325b451f9f8b65bdf1d/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — risk = f(hazard, exposure, vulnerability); basis for E×V.
- **Sharma & Ravindranath (2019).** *Applying the IPCC 2014 framework for hazard-specific vulnerability assessment.* Environmental Research Communications. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a41d525c6d9258189e07368776cc6c0b/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]`
- **Fuchs et al. (2024).** *The ambiguity in IPCC's risk diagram raises explanatory challenges.* Natural Hazards. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/1ccc5d0d386b596a85919eba1200b688/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — caution on naive superposition of the components.

## C. Carbon cost at risk (Exposure = emissions × carbon price)

- **Battiston, Mandel, Monasterolo, Schütze & Visentin (2017).** *A climate stress-test of the financial system.* Nature Climate Change. + **Climate Policy Relevant Sectors (CPRS)** taxonomy. [CPRS project](https://www.df.uzh.ch/en/people/professor/battiston/projects/CPRS.html) `[WEB]` — carbon-cost shock = emissions × carbon-price scenario.
- **Bolton & Kacperczyk (2023).** *Global pricing of carbon-transition risk.* The Journal of Finance. [link](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13272) `[WEB]` — carbon premium rises with policy stringency (emissions × policy interact).

## D. ETS, CBAM & embodied carbon in trade (the two priced legs)

- **Rocchi, Campo Lobato, Di Bella & Bosetti (2026).** *Expanding carbon pricing boundaries and the EU CBAM: insights into China and India.* Journal of Cleaner Production. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4997200) · [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652626001915) `[WEB]` — FIGARO/FIDELIO MRIO; the **CMCC** reference for the CBAM/embodied-trade approach.
- **Amendola (2025).** *Winners and losers of the EU CBAM. An intra-EU issue?* Energy Economics. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/448d1ffbe1985f3abca01ec229cc2525/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — MRIO; intra-EU redistribution.
- **Clora et al. (2023).** *Alternative carbon border adjustment mechanisms in the EU.* Energy Policy. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/cbcc343e911252d9b9d1030c6f05eb53/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]` — Scope-1 vs Scope-1&2 import-intensity basis (a modelling choice for the CBAM leg).
- **Zhong & Pei (2023).** *Carbon border adjustment mechanism: a systematic literature review.* Climate Policy. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/681af0cc27b25452ae64d78e18544495/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]`
- **Böhringer et al. (2022).** *Potential impacts and challenges of border carbon adjustments.* Nature Climate Change. [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/faf907ce9138522a94d4141851a53133/?utm_source=claude_desktop) `[ACAD]`
- Data/instruments: **EC — CBAM Reg. (EU) 2023/956** [link](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en); **EEA EU ETS data viewer** (Union Registry: verified emissions, free allocation) [link](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/maps-and-charts/emissions-trading-viewer-1-dashboards); **OECD Effective Carbon Rates 2025** [link](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/effective-carbon-rates-2025_a5a5d71f-en.html); **FIGARO** (Eurostat MRIO); **FIDELIO** (JRC) [link](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC141962).

## E. Transition risk & the "uneven transition" (Exposure/risk motivation)

- **Eicke & Goldthau (2021).** *Are we at risk of an uneven low-carbon transition? Assessing evidence from a mixed-method elite study.* Environmental Science & Policy 124, 370–379. DOI 10.1016/j.envsci.2021.07.009. `[FULL]` (PDF reviewed) — **conceptual backbone**: uneven transition → economic instability (stranded assets + competitiveness) + international tensions; **finance access** and **CBAM/carbon-content** as risk drivers. *Use:* Introduction motivation + lit review; country-level & perception-based, so position your quantitative NUTS-2 × sector index as its operationalisation. *(Earlier web search mis-identified this ID as an unrelated seabirds paper — disregarded.)*
  - References worth pulling from inside it: **Weko, Eicke, Marian & Apergi (2020),** *The Global Impacts of an EU CBAM,* IASS (CBAM impacts; Eicke co-author); **Schmidt (2014),** *Low-carbon investment risks and de-risking,* Nature Climate Change (finance dimension); **Sovacool et al. (2020),** *The decarbonization divide,* Global Environmental Change; **Pauw et al. (2020),** *Conditional NDCs in the Paris Agreement,* Climate Policy — **note: Pieter Pauw is the handling editor.** `[FULL — from its reference list]`

## F. Transition finance / investment needs (the finance dimension)

- **McCollum et al. (2018).** *Energy investment needs for fulfilling the Paris Agreement and achieving the SDGs.* Nature Energy 3(7), 589–599. DOI 10.1038/s41560-018-0179-z. `[FULL]` (PDF reviewed) — **lead reference for the finance dimension**: six-IAM low-carbon **investment needs / gap** by scenario, with region (incl. Europe) × category breakdowns (Table 1; granular data in Supplementary Data 1, *not yet obtained*). **Bosetti** co-author (CMCC). *Caveats:* measures investment **needs**, not **access** ("where the needs are, not who pays"); macro-regional, not NUTS-2. *Use:* anchor the needs side of a **finance-gap** measure: `gap ≈ needs (McCollum-type) − available flows (InvestEU / JTM / Social Climate Fund)`.
